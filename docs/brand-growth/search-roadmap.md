## AEO and legal scope

[Google's AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are no additional technical requirements or special optimisations for AI Overviews/AI Mode. Indexing and serving are not guaranteed. Useful text, links, author attribution and accurate structured data support understanding; they do not purchase or guarantee AI visibility.

[Google Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) requires a location customers can visit or travel to customers. Hiding a home address does not make online-only delivery eligible.

Australian law review should use exact legislation and applicability: Privacy Act 1988 (including small-business exceptions and circumstances that remove them), Australian Privacy Principles, Spam Act 2003, Australian Consumer Law, Cyber Security Act 2024 where applicable, and state recording/telecom obligations if introducing call recording or voice agents. There is no single “Australian Website Privacy Act” that certifies this system. PR/residency eligibility needs qualified migration advice and should not be marketed as an outcome of SEO.

OAIC's attempted direct-marketing URL returned 404; ACMA's spam guidance URL returned 503 during this research. The [OAIC APP entrypoint](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles) and [ACMA spam guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) are follow-up sources, not legal guidance verified in this run. Do not publish claims of full compliance from this research.
