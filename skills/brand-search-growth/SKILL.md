## Workflow

1. Record the date, target domains, baseline commit and actual live checks. Inspect status codes, titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, robots, sitemaps, headings, author information and structured data. A 200 response is not proof of indexing.
2. Split audiences: personal content serves employers and technical peers; agency content serves owners of trade and home-service businesses. Salesforce administration is the immediate career focus; forward deployed engineering is a direction until supported by delivery evidence.
3. Research a keyword set by intent, location and audience. Read accessible search results and competitors' own pages. Record exact source URLs and observation dates. Identify direct service competitors separately from software alternatives. Label guessed demand and priority as hypotheses. Do not invent monthly volume, keyword difficulty, rankings, market share or competitors' outcomes.
4. Read current Google Search Central AI and spam guidance. AEO means clear answers, accessible text, useful structure and attributable evidence. Do not promise AI citations, indexing, rich results or global dominance. Do not describe llms.txt or FAQ markup as an AI indexing requirement. General commercial sites should not expect FAQ rich results.
5. Choose one useful topic per audience. Start with a direct answer, use question headings when helpful and show actual evidence. Preserve simulation, planned and AI-assisted labels. Never invent clients, credentials, reviews, certifications, years of IT delivery or financial results. Describe customer-service history separately from technical experience.
6. Use the content prompts in `references/content-prompts.md`. Include a related project/service link and a useful next step. Avoid keyword stuffing, copied vendor comparisons and bulk AI pages without original value. Date vendor observations; check currency, tax and usage limits before quoting prices.
7. Add matching BlogPosting data and a visible author, canonical URL and publication date. Escape `<` in JSON-LD. Add images only when they explain the content; give them accurate alt text. Keep images responsive. Preserve each host's separate content and metadata. Read the installed Next.js docs before framework changes.
8. Produce one blog-to-social pack: carousel outline, infographic, caption, short video script and link. Label a script as a script, a generated image as an image and rendered video as video. Never claim that media was published unless its public URL is checked.
9. For citations, verify each directory's eligibility, privacy choices and fees. Online-only businesses are not eligible for Google Business Profile solely by hiding an address. Do not invent a storefront or expose a home address. Consider Yellow Pages and other directories only after checking current terms. Use consistent verified business details.
10. Earn links through original tutorials, genuine community participation and relevant directories. Do not buy ranking links, create fake reviews, spam comments or claim that social links guarantee ranking improvements.
11. Make follow-up consent-based. A visit is not permission for ongoing marketing. Define purpose, consent evidence, withdrawal, retention and stop conditions before integrating messaging or tracking. Review the Privacy Act 1988 and its applicability, Australian Privacy Principles, Spam Act 2003, Australian Consumer Law and any relevant state recording, telecom or Cyber Security Act 2024 obligations for the actual service. Do not call a template legally compliant or substitute a disclaimer for operational controls.
12. Validate lint/build and relevant host-isolation, metadata and content behavior. Save sanitized evidence. Push task-only changes through a branch/PR, verify the remote SHA and report exactly what is prepared, live, unverified or blocked. User authority governs publication; do not send unsolicited messages merely because this skill suggests outreach.
